# 01 — Product Vision

## 1. The product
Working title: **Kingdoms Conquest**.

Genre: real-time territorial strategy + formation command + social multiplayer.

The map represents a fictional country/realm divided into provinces. Each province contains a strategically meaningful castle. Castles generate the resources required to maintain and expand armies.

Players enter a lobby, form parties, select a map/mode, and join a 2/3/4-team battle.

## 2. Why it can work on Roblox
The strongest Roblox loop is not “deep strategy”; it is:
**visible progress + social competition + immediate action + collection/progression + replayability**.

Therefore the strategy layer must remain readable:
- one resource family in MVP;
- three troop archetypes;
- a small number of formations;
- a compact map;
- short matches;
- strong territory visualization.

## 3. Unique product combination
The differentiator is the chain:
Country → provinces → castles → four gates → contested capture → income → army creation → formations → real-time battle → territory changes.

No single competitor should be copied. We borrow generic genre patterns and create original expression.

## 4. Player promise
Within the first 90 seconds a new player should:
1. know where their castle is;
2. see an enemy/neutral objective;
3. understand how to move an army;
4. understand what capture does;
5. perform one meaningful action.

## 5. Design pillars
### Pillar A — Battlefield clarity
The player can read ownership, danger, objectives, and army state without opening a menu.

### Pillar B — Commander fantasy
The player is not a soldier. They are the commander. Their highest-value actions are orders, positioning, timing, and choosing where to commit forces.

### Pillar C — Territory has economic meaning
Owning land is not cosmetic. Provinces fund the war.

### Pillar D — Short feedback loops
Every action has visible feedback: banners, map color, income ticks, unit response, capture meter, combat impact.

### Pillar E — Premium restraint
No excessive particles, neon gradients, random UI cards, or asset-store soup. Consistency beats quantity.

## 6. Target first-session experience
0:00–0:20 lobby → choose team/party.
0:20–0:45 spawn → camera establishes territory.
0:45–1:30 first command → army moves.
1:30–3:00 first contested objective.
3:00–6:00 first major battle.
6:00–12:00 strategic snowball/counterattack.
12:00–16:00 decisive phase.
Target MVP match: 10–16 minutes.

## 7. Anti-frustration principles
- No long empty travel.
- No capture reset caused by a single accidental touch.
- No pay-to-win power.
- No invisible economy rules.
- No one-button instant victory.
- Losing a castle should hurt but should not make recovery impossible.

## 8. Success hypothesis
The product is promising if playtesters repeatedly:
- understand the next action without coaching;
- voluntarily fight over objectives;
- issue more than one formation order per battle;
- rematch after a loss;
- party up with friends;
- describe the territory changes as “my war/story”.
